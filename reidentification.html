The question

How can a distributed camera system recognise a previously seen person or vehicle while keeping the model, communication and persistent state working together?

What I built

I combined camera-side detection and feature extraction with a central matching server. The server stores identities and sightings in PostgreSQL and reconstructs the identity gallery after a restart.

I wrote the database schema and queries for storing, updating and retrieving history. I also took responsibility for planning the server and data handling, discussing those decisions with my supervisor.

A change that mattered

When the re-identification results were weaker than I expected, I looked more carefully at the data, explored other approaches and asked more experienced people for advice. Adding YOLO detection before re-identification improved the results.

Thinking beyond the model

Raw video stays local, while embeddings and thumbnail crops are sent to the server. This is a specific data-handling choice, not a claim of anonymity or complete privacy protection. Matching, storage and network capacity still constrain the system as it grows.

What I took from it

This project brought together my electrical-engineering, embedded-systems and AI background. I learned to think about recovery, data storage and the behaviour of the whole system alongside the recognition model.

Research direction: distributed visual recognition and the engineering choices that shape practical evaluation.
